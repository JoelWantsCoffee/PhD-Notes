### Knots

Recall that the category of smooth manifolds $\mathrm{SMan}$ is enriched over $\mathrm{Top}$. Let $\mathscr{K}$ be the set (monoid) of knots; $\pi_0\Big(\mathrm{Emb}(S^1, \mathbb{R}^3)\Big)$, where $\mathrm{Emb}(S^1, \mathbb{R}^3) \hookrightarrow \mathrm{SMan}(S^1, \mathbb{R}^3)$ is the subspace topology on the set monos.

The set of knots becomes a monoid under connect sum `picture`; the is horrible to define.







Turns out it's isomorphic to the free commutative monoid on infinite generators $(\mathbb{Z}^+, \times)$. So either knots are boring, or the category of monoids isn't rich enough to describe knots.

Fix a ring $R$ and consider the monoid algebra $R[\mathscr{K}] \in \mathsf{Mon}(\mathsf{Mod}_R)$. We're going to install a ***filtration*** on this algebra.

### Filtrations

Suppose $\mathscr{C}$ is a category. The category of filtrations $\mathsf{Fil}(\mathscr{C})$ is defined as the full subcategory of monomorphism preserving functors $[\mathbb{N}_\geq, \mathscr{C}]_\mathrm{mono}$. We usually insist that $\mathscr{C}$ is an abelian category.
$$
A_0 \hookleftarrow A_1 \hookleftarrow A_2 \hookleftarrow \cdots
$$
Example: graded filtrations (maps are biproduct inclusions) $A \oplus B \oplus C \hookleftarrow B \oplus C \hookleftarrow C \hookleftarrow 0 \hookleftarrow \cdots$. DRAW CONCENTRIC CIRCLES. More generally, there are graded things:
$$[\mathbb{N}, \mathscr{A}] \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Gr}} \mathsf{Fil}(\mathscr{A})$$
Okay, the filtration on knots: we define the crossing change surgery by weeping gnashing of teeth. 







It's a gnarly operation, very partial. $[K] \sim [K']$ if $K = C_{p,q}K'$. From this relation we generate an ideal $\mathcal{I} = \langle K - K' \,|\, K \sim K' \rangle$ of $R[\mathscr{K}]$, that is THE IDEAL OF DOUBLE POINTS. From this ideal, we get a filtration: and object of $\mathsf{Fil}(\mathsf{Mod}_R)$. Note $R[\mathscr{K}]/\mathcal{I}$ is a very boring object.
$$
R[\mathscr{K}] \hookleftarrow \mathcal{I} \hookleftarrow \mathcal{I}^2 \hookleftarrow \mathcal{I}^3 \hookleftarrow \cdots
$$
This is called the Vassiliev filtration. The idea is that this filtration captures the ugly topological information, so I guess it makes sense that relation that defines it is gross.

***lemma.*** If $\mathscr{A}$ is abelian then $\mathsf{Fil}(\mathscr{A})$ is preabelian.
***proof.*** Obviously additive, the inclusion functor $\mathsf{Fil}(\mathscr{A}) \to [\mathbb{N}, \mathscr{A}]$ has an adjoint which lets us complete kernels / cokernels.

Question: graded filtrations are nice. Is $\mathscr{K}$ a graded filtration?
### Grade-ification
Alright son, we're going to define the associated graded, or grade-ification, functor $\mathrm{gr} : \mathsf{Fil}(\mathscr{A}) \to \mathsf{Fil}(\mathscr{A})$.
$$\mathrm{gr}:\mathsf{Fil}(\mathscr{A}) \xrightarrow{\text{split}} [\mathbb{N}, \mathsf{Mor}(\mathscr{A})] \xrightarrow{[\mathbb{N},\,\mathrm{cod\,cok}]} [\mathbb{N}, \mathscr{A}] \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Gr}} \mathsf{Fil}(\mathscr{A})$$
Okay, so what does this functor do? $(\operatorname{gr}A)_n = \prod_{i \geq n} A_i/A_{i+1}$. Okay... how do we understand $\operatorname{gr} R[\mathscr{K}]$?

The $i^{th}$ component of the filtration should have $i$ double points, and we quotient by all further crossing changes.
### Formality Isomorphisms

Is $R[\mathscr{K}] \cong \operatorname{gr} R[\mathscr{K}]$?

Can we show that the limit $\displaystyle\lim_{k} R[\mathscr{K}]/\mathcal{I}^k \;\cong\; \operatorname{gr}R[\mathscr{K}]$ is the gradificiation? 
***lemma.*** It is equivalent to find a $\mathrm{gr}$-homomorphic expansion over $R[\mathscr{K}]$.
